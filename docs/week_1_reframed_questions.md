# Week 1: Reframing Marketing Questions into Causal Questions

## 1. Why Reframing Matters

Most marketing questions are framed in terms of:

* performance
* attribution
* observed outcomes

However, these do not capture causality.

If the question is wrong:

* the analysis will be misleading
* the decision will be suboptimal

Reframing ensures we are solving the **right problem**:

> measuring incremental impact, not just observed performance

---

## 2. Examples of Reframing

### Example 1: Paid Social

**Weak Question:**
Did paid social drive leads?

**Better Question:**
How many leads would NOT have occurred without paid social exposure?

**Why it’s better:**
Focuses on incrementality rather than total volume

---

### Example 2: Paid Search

**Weak Question:**
Is paid search our best-performing channel?

**Better Question:**
How much incremental revenue does paid search generate compared to organic demand?

**Why it’s better:**
Separates demand capture from demand creation

---

### Example 3: Retargeting

**Weak Question:**
Is retargeting delivering strong ROI?

**Better Question:**
What proportion of retargeting conversions would have happened without retargeting?

**Why it’s better:**
Directly addresses selection bias

---

### Example 4: YouTube / Video

**Weak Question:**
Why does YouTube show low attribution performance?

**Better Question:**
What is the incremental impact of YouTube on downstream conversions across channels?

**Why it’s better:**
Accounts for upper-funnel effects

---

### Example 5: Email Campaigns

**Weak Question:**
Did this email campaign perform well?

**Better Question:**
How did the email campaign change user behavior compared to not sending the email?

**Why it’s better:**
Introduces a counterfactual comparison

---

## 3. Business-Level Questions

### Budget Allocation

**Weak Question:**
Where should we invest more budget?

**Better Question:**
Which channels deliver the highest incremental return at the margin?

---

### Channel Performance

**Weak Question:**
Which channel drives the most conversions?

**Better Question:**
Which channel drives the most incremental conversions after accounting for baseline demand?

---

## 4. Common Patterns

Weak questions usually:

* rely on observed outcomes
* ignore counterfactuals
* assume causality

Strong questions:

* explicitly define a counterfactual
* focus on incremental impact
* enable experimental or causal measurement

---

## 5. Key Takeaway

Better analysis starts with better questions.

In marketing measurement:

* the goal is not to explain what happened
* the goal is to understand what would have happened otherwise

This shift is essential for making reliable decisions.
